# The Great Stink — original script v1 (2026-11-07)

Facts: Hansard HC Deb 15 Jul 1858 (Disraeli, "Stygian pool", £3m, 3d rate/40 yrs); royal assent 2 Aug 1858 = 18 days; Faraday 1855 "a real sewer"; Godwin "more than six feet deep"; ~2.5-3m population, ~200,000 cesspits, ~360 sewers (1856); lime chloride on curtains + 200-250 long tons of lime at £1,500/week; no cholera outbreak in London in 1858 (outbreaks: 1831-32, 1848-49, 1853-54, 1866); miasma theory (Chadwick: "all smell is disease"); Bazalgette 82 miles intercepting + ~1,100 miles street sewers, 318m bricks, Portland cement, 1859-75; Metropolis Water Act 1852 moved intakes above Teddington Lock from 31 Aug 1855 — BEFORE the sewers; 118F is a sun-exposed thermometer, not air temperature.

You are standing inside the British Parliament, and you cannot breathe. It is July 1858, the windows are open, and the air coming through them tastes like a sewer. Because it is one. London has two and a half million people, and every sink, every toilet, every slaughterhouse in the city drains into a single river. The Thames. It is not water anymore. It is a six foot deep soup of untreated sewage, cooking in the summer heat, and Parliament was built directly beside it. Members soak the curtains in lime, hold handkerchiefs over their faces, and seriously discuss abandoning London for St Albans. The newspapers called it the Great Stink, and it was strong enough to empty a debating chamber. Meanwhile, London drinks from that same river, which is why cholera has killed tens of thousands of people over the last twenty years. Then, after decades of refusing to pay for sewers, they do something extraordinary. They approve three million pounds in eighteen days. They hire an engineer called Joseph Bazalgette to dig eighty two miles of brick tunnels beneath the city. Now here is the part that makes no sense. They believed the smell itself was the poison. It was not. The foul air was not making anybody sick, and that summer killed almost nobody. But the sewers worked anyway, because they did not just carry away the stink, they carried away the water that was killing people. And London was saved by a mistake.
